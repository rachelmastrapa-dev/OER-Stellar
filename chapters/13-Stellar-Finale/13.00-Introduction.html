Introduction

In this chapter we will discuss the end-states of stars. Just as the length of a stars life is dependent on the stars initial mass, what are star looks like at the end of its life also depends on mass. Almost all stars end in a bang, but the size of the explosion also depends on its initial mass, but also whether it has on or more companion stars.

Learning Objectives
  • Distinguish the mass range of stars that will become white dwarfs
  • Describe the interior of a massive star before a supernova
  • Describe the observed features of SN 1987A both before and after the supernova
  • Explain the research method that led to the discovery of neutron stars
  • Describe the features of a neutron star that allow it to be detected as a pulsar
  • List the observational evidence that links pulsars and neutron stars to supernovae
  • Describe the kind of binary star system that leads to a nova event
  • Describe the type of binary star system that leads to a type Ia supernovae event
  • Indicate how type Ia supernovae differ from type II supernovae
